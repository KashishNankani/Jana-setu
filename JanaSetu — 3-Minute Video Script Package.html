B. COMPLETE VOICEOVER (paste directly into the voice tool)

A pipe leaks near the market. Whose job is it: municipality, water board, ward office? Each has its own app, helpline and portal.

Then comes the wait: no clear acknowledgement, no visible owner, no timeline. Citizens keep following up. And audits show tickets marked closed while the problem remains.

The issue isn't complaints. It's a journey that's fragmented, opaque, and closed on paperwork, not outcomes.

This is JanaSetu, a bridge between citizens and government. Citizens use WhatsApp, with no new app to learn. Officers use dedicated dashboards.

The citizen sends a photo, video, voice note or text, in their own language. AI identifies the problem, then asks: "Is this a water-pipeline leak?" Nothing is registered until the citizen confirms.

Next, the location: live GPS, a landmark, or a voice description, confirmed by the citizen. What, and exactly where, before anything enters the workflow.

Three citizens report the same leak. JanaSetu clusters them into one problem: one ID, one tracking link. All stay linked, and the count becomes a priority signal.

The problem goes to its responsible department first. Within each department, priority is scored using fixed, configurable weightages: reports, population affected, severity, duration. Officials get an evidence report showing why.

The government reviews the prioritised list and assigns departments. The system suggests an officer; a human makes the final call. AI then recommends how to fix it.

Every task carries a defined time limit. Need more time? The officer requests an extension with a reason, reviewed by a human. Unjustified delays follow government norms.

When an officer claims it's fixed, an upload alone isn't enough. Live-camera photos are location-checked, and AI always checks whether the evidence is usable, relevant and matches the complaint. Blank or unrelated photos don't count.

Citizens then get a WhatsApp question: is it really solved? Their answer takes priority; AI is the fallback. A "No" with new evidence reopens the case. No response? Closure follows policy, and needs AI confirmation.

If a wrong closure slips through, the same officer isn't given that problem again. We may fail once, never twice. Every action builds a record for accountable human decisions.

What's different: one WhatsApp entry point, not many portals; one problem, one link, not scattered tickets; closure checked by AI and citizens, not by an upload.

The result: citizens report once and track one status. Departments get prioritised, evidence-backed work. Duplicates shrink, delays become visible, and accountability rests on records.

JanaSetu: from complaint to verified resolution. Fail once, never twice.
